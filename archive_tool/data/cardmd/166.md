## Description

### **Problem Statement**

Baird Augustine currently manages large institutional debt deals (typically **$25 million and above**) through a manual process. Recently, they have seen a rise in smaller debt deal requests (**below $25 million**).

While these smaller transactions help build early-stage relationships with clients, they are highly **manual and time-consuming**, diverting focus from larger, more profitable deals.

The need identified during the discussion is to **automate the sub-$25 million debt deal process** — enabling efficiency, reducing manual effort, and freeing up the team’s bandwidth for high-value work.

---

### **2. Business Context**

Baird Augustine operates three main business units:

1. **Investment Banking** – Works with 33 family offices, deploying approximately $20B annually.
2. **Asymmetrical Alpha** – Hedge fund division.
3. **Focus on Risk** – Investor network hosting institutional investing events.

The automation platform will primarily support the **Investment Banking** division’s smaller-deal operations.

---

### **3. Current Workflow (Manual)**

1. Clients reach out via email or phone to request debt funding.
2. The team collects basic information manually.
3. Clients submit **bank statements**:
   - 3 months if based outside **New York or California**.
   - 4 months if based **in New York or California**.
4. An internal team member manually inputs data from the bank statements into a spreadsheet to determine the client’s debt capacity.
5. Quotes are manually requested from liquidity providers via email.
6. Once providers reply, a team member compares quotes and sends the best option to the client.
7. Deal progress is tracked manually in **Asana**.

This process is repetitive, human-dependent, and limits scalability.

---

### **4. Target Workflow (Automation Scope)**

Ryan confirmed that automation is needed only for **debt deals under $25 million**.
The **larger, complex deals** will continue to involve significant human interaction.

The automated process should begin **on Baird Augustine’s website**, allowing borrowers to self-initiate their applications online.

---

### **5. Functional Requirements (as discussed)**

#### **5.1 Client Intake Form**

- Hosted on **Baird Augustine’s website** (currently not available).
- Form to collect:
  - Loan amount range (e.g., under $5M, $5M–$10M, $10M–$25M).
  - Type of debt (e.g., Line of Credit, Supply Chain Financing, Asset-Based, Revenue-Based, SBA Loan, etc.).
- **Dynamic fields:** form expands based on user input (e.g., type of loan or business details).
- Borrowers can **upload bank statements (PDFs)** directly.
- The system should validate that clients provide:
  - 3 statements (if outside NY/CA).
  - 4 statements (if inside NY/CA).

---

#### **5.2 Data Extraction and Analysis**

- Extract relevant financial data from uploaded bank statements, including:
  - Monthly ending balances.
  - Cash inflows and outflows.
- Calculate the **debt amount the business qualifies for**, based on this extracted data.
- The goal is to replace the manual spreadsheet calculations currently done by the team.
- **Credit scoring, covenants, or financial ratio templates** may be considered later (“maybe in the future”).
- Business performance (not personal credit) should remain the key eligibility factor.

---

#### **5.3 Lender / Liquidity Provider Workflow**

- Baird Augustine works with an existing list of **liquidity providers**.
- The system should:
  - Automatically send quote requests to these providers once analysis is complete.
  - Receive and compile responses for comparison.
- A **human analyst** must still review the offers and select the best one.
- Option for manual negotiation before sending the quote to the client.

---

#### **5.4 User Roles (Defined in the Conversation)**

RoleDescription

- **Client (Borrower):** Fills the online form, uploads documents, and submits a request.
- **Internal Analyst:** Reviews system output (eligibility amount, provider quotes), validates accuracy, and communicates with the client.
- **Liquidity Provider:** Receives quote requests and sends responses (currently via email).
- **Admin (Ryan / Management)**: Oversees all processes, user permissions, and deal tracking.

---

#### **5.5 Workflow Integration**

- **Asana:**
  - Every new application should automatically create a task in Asana.
  - Tasks show deal type, amount range, and client name.
- **Google Workspace:**
  - Used for document storage and internal communication.
- **CRM:**
  - Not in use currently; no CRM integration required at this stage.

---

### **6. End-to-End Workflow (Based on Conversation)**

1. Client visits Baird Augustine’s website.
2. Client fills in the online intake form and uploads 3–4 months of bank statements.
3. System extracts and analyzes financial data.
4. System determines the maximum eligible debt amount.
5. Quote requests are sent to Baird Augustine’s listed liquidity providers.
6. Providers reply with their quotes.
7. System aggregates all quotes.
8. Analyst reviews and selects the best option (or negotiates if needed).
9. Quote is sent to the client for approval or rejection.
10. The deal is logged and tracked in **Asana**.

---

### **7. Process Rules (Confirmed in Discussion)**

- Deals **below $25 million** are the automation target.
- **Line of Credit** → fewer questions.
- **Complex debt types** → more detailed information required.
- Conditional logic within the form based on:
  - Loan amount.
  - Type of debt.
  - Borrower’s responses.
- For smaller, simpler loans (like working capital), the process can be mostly automated.
- Large, complex loans still require human intervention.
